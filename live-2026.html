ADV Gridiron Report

Hype vs. Results: What Texas and Georgia Actually Became

The public expected Texas to rise and Georgia to regress. The underlying football, and the head-to-head results, told a different story.

The narratives surrounding Texas and Georgia entering the 2025 season seemed to be moving in opposite directions.

Texas had the hype.

The beginning of the Arch Manning era created enormous expectations. After a 13-3 finish in 2024 and a trip to the College Football Playoff semifinal, the Longhorns were viewed as a program positioned to remain among college football's elite.

Georgia had the questions.

The Bulldogs were replacing Carson Beck with Gunner Stockton, their passing production was expected to change, and there was concern that Georgia's offense might take a meaningful step backward.

Then the season happened.

Using the rebuilt CFP Advantage Product A outputs, the team-level evidence reveals a much different story: Texas declined significantly across its underlying football profile. Georgia remained elite and became more efficient offensively. And when the teams met on the field, Georgia kept winning.

A Necessary Data Guardrail

Before comparing the teams, the scope of the data must be clear. The CFP Advantage team_intelligence_board figures used in this study are classified as srs_scope = pre_playoff_exact and includes_postseason = False.

That means the ADV rankings and team-strength figures represent each team's exact profile before the College Football Playoff began. Traditional statistics and final records may include postseason games, depending on the available season rows. For that reason, this should be interpreted as a comparison of team-profile direction, not a perfect quarterback attribution study.

It is also important to separate the records correctly.

The 2024 records of 13-3 for Texas and 11-3 for Georgia are full-season records. At the pre-playoff checkpoint used by the ADV board, both teams were 11-2. That distinction matters because it keeps the ADV comparison aligned with the actual data scope.

Texas Fell Off Hard

Texas did not merely experience a small decline from 2024 to 2025. Its entire team profile deteriorated.

Texas also lost 15.2 points of Defensive ADV SRS. The Longhorns became less effective on both sides of the ball.

That matters because a weaker offense can sometimes be protected by an elite defense. A declining defense can sometimes be covered by an explosive offense. Texas did not have either luxury. Both halves of the profile moved backward.

The lower figure reflects a team that created meaningful offensive pressure less consistently.

The Finish Rate Trap

The most revealing part of the Texas profile is that one important metric actually improved. Texas' Control Finish Rate rose from 83.9% in 2024 to 89.7% in 2025.

Viewed alone, that could make the offense appear better. It was not. Control Finish Rate measures how often meaningful control drives become scoring drives. It answers an important question: once an offense creates control, how often does it finish the opportunity?

But it does not tell us how often those opportunities were created. Texas finished a greater percentage of its meaningful control drives in 2025, but the Longhorns created fewer of them.

The rest of the profile shows that clearly: Control Rate declined, Offensive ADV SRS declined, total offense declined, passing production declined, rushing production declined, third-down efficiency declined, and Control Points per Offensive Drive declined.

Texas became better at finishing a smaller and less frequently created group of opportunities. That is not the same as becoming a better offense.

Imagine one offense creates ten meaningful control drives and scores on eight. A second offense creates only five and scores on all five. The second team has a perfect finish rate, but the first team applied far more pressure throughout the game.

That is why finish cannot be evaluated in isolation. Finish measures the completion of control. It does not measure the creation of control. Texas is a near-perfect example of how Finish Rate can tell the truth while still creating a misleading conclusion when separated from the rest of the framework.

Georgia's defense declined modestly, but the offense more than offset that loss. The concern was that Georgia might become less effective offensively. The profile says the opposite.

Georgia may have generated fewer passing yards, but it created control more frequently and extracted more value from each possession. That is not offensive regression. Passing Volume Is Not Offensive Control

Traditional analysis often gravitates toward passing yards, total yards, explosive plays and individual quarterback production. Those numbers matter, but they do not always explain whether an offense consistently controlled games.

Georgia's reduction in passing volume could have been interpreted as evidence that its offense had become less dangerous. The complete profile does not support that conclusion.

Georgia improved in Offensive ADV SRS, Control Rate, Points per Drive, rushing production, third-down conversion rate, red-zone touchdown rate and Control Points per Offensive Drive.

The Bulldogs generated fewer passing yards but more scoring pressure. A team does not receive additional value for accumulating yards between the 20-yard lines if the drive repeatedly stalls. The objective is not simply to gain yards. The objective is to create control, sustain it and finish it. Georgia became better at doing exactly that.

Georgia Also Settled It on the Field

The season-level profiles already tell a compelling story. The head-to-head results make it harder to dismiss. Georgia and Texas played three times across the 2024 and 2025 seasons. Georgia won all three.

On October 19, 2024, Texas entered the game ranked No. 1 and hosted Georgia in Austin. Georgia won 30-15, building a 23-0 halftime lead and handing Texas its first loss of the season.

On December 7, 2024, the teams met again in the SEC Championship Game. Georgia won 22-19 in overtime, defeating Texas for the second time that season and claiming the conference championship.

On November 15, 2025, Texas entered the rematch ranked No. 10. Georgia was ranked No. 5. The Bulldogs won 35-10 in Athens. Gunner Stockton accounted for five touchdowns, four passing and one rushing, as Georgia outscored Texas 21-0 in the fourth quarter.

Across those three meetings, Georgia outscored Texas 87-44. The average result was Georgia 29, Texas 15.

Georgia won at Texas, at a neutral site for the SEC championship, and at Georgia. It won with Carson Beck starting and with Gunner Stockton starting. The matchup results do not independently prove every part of the season-level analysis. Three games are still only three games. But they reinforce the central conclusion: the gap between the teams was not merely theoretical.

Two Teams, Two Analytical Traps

Texas shows how better finishing can hide weaker creation. Texas' higher Control Finish Rate could appear encouraging without context. But Texas created fewer meaningful opportunities, generated less pressure per drive and declined sharply in both offensive and overall ADV SRS. The finish improved. The offense did not.

Georgia shows how lower passing volume can hide greater efficiency. Georgia threw for fewer yards, but the Bulldogs became more efficient in the situations that determined games. The passing volume declined. The offense did not.

What the Narrative Got Wrong

The simplified public narrative was something like this: Texas and Arch Manning were the ascending force. Georgia and Gunner Stockton were the concern.

The CFP Advantage results tell a different story. Texas dropped from fourth to 31st in pre-playoff ADV SRS rank. Georgia moved from eighth to seventh. Texas' Offensive ADV SRS declined by 22.6 points. Georgia's improved by 4.3. Texas' Control Rate fell by 6.2 percentage points. Georgia's increased. Texas produced less offensive pressure per drive. Georgia produced more. And when the teams played, Georgia beat Texas three consecutive times.

That does not make this a direct quarterback ranking. Arch Manning did not single-handedly create every weakness in Texas' profile, just as Gunner Stockton did not single-handedly create every Georgia improvement.

Quarterback play exists within a larger structure that includes offensive line performance, rushing efficiency, receiver play, field position, play calling, opponent quality, defensive support, drive volume, third-down execution and red-zone performance.

This study is not designed to isolate every one of those variables. It is designed to answer a broader question: what did the teams actually become? The answer is clear. Texas became considerably worse. Georgia remained elite and became more efficient offensively.

Why the Control Framework Matters

Traditional statistics describe production. CFP Advantage is designed to explain how that production was created and whether it represented repeatable football control. That requires multiple layers.

Control Rate measures how often an offense generates successful control events from its eligible opportunities. Control Finish Rate measures how often meaningful control drives become scoring drives. Control Points per Offensive Drive combines control creation and scoring value to measure the repeatable pressure an offense produces with each possession. Offensive ADV SRS places that performance within an opponent-adjusted team-strength framework.

No individual metric tells the complete story. Texas proves why. Looking only at Finish Rate suggests improvement. Looking at the entire control framework reveals that the Longhorns created less, sustained less and produced less.

Georgia demonstrates the opposite problem. Looking only at passing volume suggests regression. Looking at the full profile reveals an offense that became more efficient, more successful in critical situations and more productive on a possession-by-possession basis.

The Final Take

This is not ultimately a story about two quarterbacks. It is a story about the distance between hype and results.

Texas entered 2025 with the excitement, but its underlying profile collapsed. Georgia entered with the questions, but its offense improved. Texas finished a higher percentage of its meaningful control opportunities but created far fewer of them. Georgia passed for fewer yards but generated more scoring pressure on each possession.

Then Georgia beat Texas again, its third consecutive victory over the Longhorns across two seasons.

Texas looked like the ascending team but became less effective. Georgia looked like the uncertain team but remained elite. That is the difference between following a narrative and auditing the football underneath it.

And that is exactly the kind of story CFP Advantage was built to uncover.
