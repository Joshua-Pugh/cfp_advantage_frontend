Lesson 01 Why I Built CFP Advantage

Football Question

Why are good teams actually good, and why do some final scores fail to explain the game we just watched?

Why This Question Matters

College football fans already know the scoreboard matters. The problem is that the scoreboard can leave out the shape of the game. A team can win by three touchdowns without feeling dominant, or lose a close game after controlling long stretches of the afternoon.

That is the argument CFP Advantage was built to investigate. Not just who won. Not just who moved up in a poll. Why did the game move the way it did?

The Investigation

The first instinct was to test the obvious answers: yards, points, third downs, explosive plays, turnovers, and time of possession. Some mattered. None explained the whole game by themselves.

So the model moved closer to the field. Every play. Every drive. Every possession. Every chance for a team to create value before the final score had a chance to speak.

What We Found

The strongest Product A signals were not just scoreboard totals. They were patterns of control, pressure, finishing, denial, schedule context, and team strength. The best teams kept producing football advantages that survived different opponents and game states.

What It Means

CFP Advantage is not just asking who won. It is asking what a team consistently proved on the field, and whether that performance looks repeatable.

Football Example

Two teams both score 35. One creates long, repeatable scoring drives. The other scores on a return, a short field, and one broken play. The scoreboard matches, but the football profile does not.

Reading CFP Advantage

Read CFP Advantage as a football explanation system first. Rankings, projections, and profiles all come from the same question: what did the team consistently prove on the field?

Lesson 02 What Is Football Advantage?

Football Question

Every fan has watched a team dominate a stretch of football before the score finally changed. Where was that advantage coming from?

Why This Question Matters

Football is not decided only by scoring plays. It is built from small wins that stack together: a seven-yard gain, an early first down, a better field-position exchange, or a defense staying on the field longer than it wants to.

None of those moments guarantee points. But together they tilt the field. Advantage usually arrives before the scoreboard confirms it.

The Investigation

CFP Advantage looked for what happened before offenses consistently scored. The answer was not simply raw yardage. Successful offenses were putting defenses into worse and worse situations.

They stayed ahead of schedule, extended possessions, improved field position, and forced defenses to defend the full playbook.

What We Found

Advantage was usually built one small football edge at a time. Those edges did not always become points, but they made points far more likely.

What It Means

When CFP Advantage says advantage, think pressure, field position, schedule, and uncomfortable football for the opponent. The scoreboard usually comes later.

Football Example

One offense gains 20 yards on a busted coverage before punting. Another gains seven, then six, then converts third-and-one, then crosses midfield. Similar yardage. Very different advantage.

Important caution

Advantage is not the same as total yards. A drive can gain yards late, behind schedule, or after the game state has already changed.

Lesson 03 Why The Box Score Can Lie

Football Question

Why can two similar box scores describe two completely different football games?

Why This Question Matters

Box scores compress football into totals. They tell you how many yards, points, first downs, and turnovers happened. They do not always tell you where those plays happened, how repeatable the success was, or whether the offense was actually in command.

The Investigation

The model tested whether familiar totals could explain game control on their own. They helped, but the same totals kept showing up in very different game scripts.

The missing piece was context: when the yards happened, how drives formed, whether points came from sustained pressure, and whether the opponent was actually forced into stress.

What We Found

Traditional stats are useful evidence, but they are not the whole story. They become much more powerful when placed beside control, pressure, drive finish, and opponent context.

What It Means

CFP Advantage does not throw out the box score. It asks whether the box score is telling the truth, hiding a warning sign, or missing a team that controlled more than the final numbers show.

Reading CFP Advantage

Use traditional stats as comparison evidence, not the entire answer. The Contextual Football Profile shows the deeper shape behind those totals.

Lesson 04 What Is Control?

Football Question

When has an offense created meaningful pressure on a drive?

Why This Question Matters

Possession by itself is not control. A team can hold the ball, gain a few yards, and still never force the defense into real danger. CFP Advantage needed a way to separate empty possession from meaningful football pressure.

The Investigation

The model studied the shape of drives that became meaningful. It looked at early first downs, explosive involvement, short fields, drive length, field position, and whether control was being earned or gifted.

What We Found

The model found a coach-friendly pattern: meaningful control usually looks like good football, not a random gift.

91-93%

of meaningful control drives earned an early first down.

62-67%

included an explosive play, but explosives alone did not define control.

~14%

started on a short field, so most control was earned rather than gifted.

What It Means

Control usually starts with staying ahead of the chains. Explosive plays help, but they do not explain everything. Most control is earned through real drive quality.

Important caution

Control is a drive-quality idea. It does not mean the team scored, and it does not mean the drive was perfect.

Lesson 05 Control Creation

Football Question

Can this offense keep putting the defense in trouble?

Why This Question Matters

Finishing matters, but an offense cannot finish opportunities it never creates. A team that only creates two meaningful drives can look efficient in a small sample and still lack a sustainable winning profile.

The Investigation

Control Creation measures how often an offense turns possessions into meaningful control drives. It is a foundation metric because it tells us how much opportunity the offense manufactures before finishing even enters the conversation.

What We Found

Creation and denial became foundational parts of the Control Framework because they describe repeatable football skill. Finish and resistance matter too, but creation tells you whether the offense keeps applying pressure possession after possession.

What It Means

Control Creation is the offense's opportunity engine. Higher creation means more drives become dangerous before the scoreboard ever gets involved.

Reading CFP Advantage

On team and matchup pages, stronger Control Creation means the offense is more consistently building drives that put pressure on the opponent.

Lesson 06 Control Finish Rate

Football Question

When the offense creates meaningful control, does it turn those chances into points?

Why This Question Matters

Some offenses move the ball but stall. Others may not create endless chances, but when they get control, they cash in. CFP Advantage needed a metric that separated creating opportunity from finishing opportunity.

Formula

Control Finish Rate = scoring control drives / meaningful control drives.

The Investigation

Control Finish Rate is not red-zone efficiency. Red zone efficiency begins with field location. Control Finish Rate begins with drive quality. It asks what happened after the offense established meaningful control.

What We Found

The two are related, but they are not the same. In CFP Advantage audits, Control Finish Rate had only a moderate relationship with red-zone scoring rate and red-zone touchdown rate.

~0.39

correlation with red-zone scoring rate.

~0.35

correlation with red-zone touchdown rate.

What It Means

Red zone stats ask what happened near the goal line. Control Finish Rate asks whether meaningful drives became points, even if the decisive part of the drive started earlier.

Important caution

A high finish rate needs sample context. Finishing 2 of 2 control drives is not the same as finishing 8 of 10.

Lesson 07 Control Pressure Per Offensive Drive

Football Question

How much sustainable scoring pressure does this offense create each time it gets the ball?

Why This Question Matters

Points per game tells you what reached the scoreboard. It does not always tell you how much of that scoring came from repeatable offensive pressure instead of short fields, returns, broken plays, or game-state noise.

Bridge

Control Pressure Per Offensive Drive = Control Creation Rate x Points Per Control Drive.

The Investigation

This is not literal scoreboard points per drive. It is an ADV-derived pressure estimate that spreads control-created scoring value across every offensive possession.

What We Found

A value around 3.5 does not mean the team scores a touchdown every drive. A touchdown every drive would be near 7.0. A 3.5 means elite sustained pressure: across ten offensive drives, the offense is creating roughly 35 control-pressure points.

Under 1.5Limited
1.5-2.3Average
2.4-3.0Strong
3.1+Elite

What It Means

Control Pressure Per Offensive Drive is best read as pressure per possession. It helps explain whether scoring is being created by the offense's normal drive quality or by less repeatable events.

Reading CFP Advantage

Compare it with points per game. Pressure is the engine; points are the result. When they disagree, the scoreboard may be running hotter or colder than the team's underlying control profile.
